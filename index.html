The Hidden Cost of Cracking India's Toughest Exams

An anonymous survey of 335 JEE and NEET aspirants reveals the mental, physical, and social toll of a system that prizes results above wellbeing.

80%
of respondents experienced burnout during preparation — yet only 26% would choose the same path again.

Methodology

How the data was collected and analysed.

This independent, anonymous survey was designed to quantify the costs of preparing for India's JEE and NEET exams. 335 current and former aspirants responded between March and May 2026.

The survey was distributed online through student forums and social media. All responses were anonymous, and no personal identifying information was collected.

Limitations: Self‑reported data, non‑random sample. Findings are exploratory and not generalisable to the entire population.
  • Sample size 335
  • Period March – May 2026
  • Distribution Online (social media, forums)
  • Anonymity Fully anonymous
  • Age range 16–21 (majority 17–19)
  • Funding Independent, no institutional backing

Key Findings

Five patterns that emerged from the data.

01

Parental Pressure & Burnout

The relationship between family expectations and student mental health.
“As parental pressure increases, burnout rates climb sharply — reaching 93% among those with very high pressure.”
Students with very high parental pressure were 93% likely to experience burnout — vs 60% with low pressure.
Source: Independent Survey, March–May 2026 · n=335
02

Parental Pressure & Career Satisfaction

Long‑term satisfaction after the preparation journey.
“Higher parental pressure correlates with lower career satisfaction, dropping from 2.72 to 2.09 on a 5‑point scale.”
Career satisfaction falls as pressure rises — from 2.72 (low) to 2.09 (very high).
Source: Independent Survey, March–May 2026 · n=335
03

Non‑Academic Skills & Long‑Term Outcomes

Whether developing skills outside academics influenced later satisfaction.
“Students who developed non‑academic skills reported higher career satisfaction and social confidence — gaps of 0.6 and 1.0 points respectively.”
Those with non‑academic skills scored 2.87 vs 2.27 on career satisfaction, and 3.31 vs 2.33 on social confidence.
Source: Independent Survey, March–May 2026 · n=335
04

Years of Preparation & Regret

How extended preparation relates to regret after the journey.
“Regret increases with preparation years: 80% of those with 3+ years regret the path, compared to 45% with no drop year.”
80% of students who spent 3+ years preparing reported regret — the highest of any group.
Source: Independent Survey, March–May 2026 · n=335
05

Even Among Those Who Made It

Success did not eliminate the psychological cost.
“Only 8 of 30 IIT/AIIMS students would recommend the preparation path — success doesn’t erase the cost.”

Discussion

What the findings suggest about the wider system.

Implications

The data consistently show that high parental pressure, extended preparation time, and a narrow focus on academic achievement are associated with poorer long‑term wellbeing — even among those who succeed. This challenges the prevailing narrative that sacrifice is a necessary price for success.

Policymakers, educators, and parents should consider interventions that reduce pressure, encourage holistic development, and provide mental health support throughout the preparation journey.

Limitations

This is an exploratory, self‑reported survey with a non‑random sample. The findings are not generalisable to all aspirants. Selection bias may overrepresent those with strong opinions or negative experiences. Causal claims cannot be made from this correlational data.

Future work should use representative sampling, longitudinal designs, and objective measures of wellbeing to strengthen the evidence base.

Ask the Dataset

AI‑powered research assistant

Ask the Dataset is an AI research companion built on this survey. It draws from all 335 responses, including 146 anonymous open-ended reflections, to identify patterns, compare groups, explain findings, and surface relevant student quotes.

If you're currently preparing for JEE or NEET, you can ask about challenges you're facing. Rather than offering generic advice, Ask the Dataset grounds its responses in the experiences shared by students who faced similar situations — helping connect your questions to real voices from the survey.
